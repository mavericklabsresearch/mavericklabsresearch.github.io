What AI is, in words a family can actually use

Artificial intelligence sounds like magic and is usually sold like magic. It isn't. This page explains, in plain language, what today's AI systems really are, how an assistive AI for a child would work, and where the honest edges are.

✦ The basicsAI is pattern-learning software

Most modern AI systems are models: programs that learn patterns from very large amounts of examples — text, pictures, sounds — and then use those patterns to produce new text, pictures, sounds, or decisions. A language model, for instance, has learned which words tend to follow which other words, so fluently that its replies can sound thoughtful. Sounding thoughtful and being thoughtful are different things, and keeping them separate in your mind is the single most useful AI skill a parent can have.

Three consequences follow from how these systems are built:

One

They don't know — they match

An AI does not look facts up in a verified store unless it was specifically built to. By default it produces what usually fits — which is why it can state false things in a confident, polished voice.

Two

They are only as good as their examples

Patterns learned from skewed examples come out skewed. A system trained mostly on typical speakers may serve a child with atypical speech poorly — unless it was deliberately designed and tested for that child.

Three

They don't understand your child

An AI can respond to information about a child. That is not the same as knowing them, caring about them, or being responsible for them. Responsibility always stays with people.

✦ Assistive AIHow an assistive system works

Assistive technology is any tool that helps a person do what they want to do. Assistive AI adds adaptation: instead of one fixed setting for everyone, the system adjusts to the individual — reading text aloud in a voice and pace a particular child follows, turning pictures into descriptions, simplifying language, remembering a routine, or guiding a crossing step by step. The honest promise of assistive AI is fit: help shaped to one person, updated as they grow. Whether any given system actually delivers that fit is an empirical question — which is why we insist on testing, and why our Benefits & Limits page gives both sides equal room.

✦ Words, decodedA small glossary

Plain-language glossary

ModelThe learned pattern-matcher at the heart of an AI system.
TrainingThe process of showing a model examples until it internalizes their patterns.
PromptWhat you type or say to an AI to get a response.
HallucinationWhen an AI confidently produces something false. A design property, not a rare bug.
PersonalizationAdjusting a system's behaviour to one individual — powerful, and the reason privacy matters.
EmbodimentGiving a system a physical body — a robot, a wearable, a speaker — so it can sense and act in the world.
GuardrailsRules and separate safety systems that limit what the AI is allowed to do, no matter what it "wants" to output.
