Questions to ask any assistive AI — including ours

You don't need to be an engineer to evaluate an AI product for your child. You need good questions and the confidence to walk away from bad answers. These checklists are yours to use on any vendor — us included, when our turn comes.

✦ Checklist oneTen questions for any assistive AI

Ask before you buy, download, or enrol

1What exactly does it do — and what does it not do?
2Was it tested with children like mine — same disabilities, same ages? May I see the results?
3What happens when it gets something wrong? How will I know?
4What data about my child does it collect, where is it stored, and who can see it?
5Can I see, export, and permanently delete my child's data?
6Does it work offline, or does my child's voice/data leave the device?
7Who is responsible when it makes a harmful mistake — a named human, a company, a process?
8Does it encourage my child to come back to people, or to stay with the machine?
9Can I limit it — time, topics, contacts — and do the limits actually hold?
10What does it cost over a year, and what happens to my child's setup if I stop paying?

✦ Checklist twoRed flags

Be wary of any product that: promises outcomes ("will teach your child to…") without published evidence; describes itself as your child's friend, companion, or confidant; can't explain its data practices in plain words; has no way for a human to review or override it; markets urgency to frightened parents; or treats your questions as hostility. Good tools welcome the ten questions above.

✦ At home & schoolHabits that help, whatever the tool

The American Academy of Pediatrics' guidance for families centres on habits, not fear: make a family media plan together, keep some times and places screen-free, use technology with your child rather than only monitoring it, and talk regularly about what they encounter. Their summary is worth ten minutes: AAP: 12 tips to help children develop healthy digital habits. For assistive technology specifically, add one habit of your own: periodically ask whether the tool is building your child's independence — and be honest about the answer together.
