The MAVY Charter: the floor that cannot be lowered

This Charter is the public floor under every individual MAVY trust contract: the commitments that no contract, update, or business decision may loosen. An individual contract — one per child, private, written with their family — inherits everything here and may only make it stricter. Where the two ever conflict, the stricter term governs.

Read this first — what this document is, and is not. This is Draft v0.1, published for public comment. MAVY is at an early research stage: there is no product available, no enrolment, and our current research involves no children. Nothing here is yet in force, and nothing here is a promise about a product's performance. Before any version of this Charter becomes binding (v1.0), it will be reviewed by an attorney and by reviewers from the disability community it governs. We publish the draft now because trust terms written after a system is built are written around the system — and families deserve to see, and question, ours first.

✦ Article 1The child is a party, not a data subject

Every MAVY relationship is governed by an individual trust contract whose parties are the child, their parent or guardian, and Maverick Labs as operator. The child assents in age-appropriate form and is heard as they grow. Clinicians and educators author the child's requirements and witness the contract, with standing to trigger a review when the requirements no longer fit the child.

✦ Article 2Permissions are enumerated; prohibitions are absolute

A MAVY system may do only what its individual contract expressly permits. Anything not permitted is not permitted. And some things are prohibited in this Charter itself — beneath this floor no individual contract may go: the system must never act outside its contracted permissions, never allow its generative side to override the independent safety controls, and never continue operating after revocation.

✦ Article 3Safety stands apart

The generative intelligence is separated from deterministic safety controls that do not learn and do not take instructions from it. Each individual contract names the exact version of the safety architecture it relies on. If that architecture cannot keep a prohibition in the contract, that is a breach of the contract — not a technical footnote.

✦ Article 4The data covenant

A child's data is a trust, not a resource. The floor for every contract: collect the minimum the purpose requires; process on the child's own hardware wherever possible; the family can see what is held, export it, and delete it — really delete it, verifiably. A child's data is never sold, and never used to train models for other children without separate, explicit agreement.

✦ Article 5What it did must be establishable

Oversight is a right, not a courtesy. Families and their witnesses can review a record of what the system did — a record kept in a form that can be checked after the fact, not merely asserted by us. Reviews happen on a schedule written into each contract, and a witness's statement that the requirements no longer fit triggers one.

✦ Article 6Failure behaviour is contracted

When the system is wrong, uncertain, or at the edge of its permissions, the contracted behaviour is: stop, hand to a human, and disclose. Failure is handled by the contract's terms, never improvised in the moment.

✦ Article 7The contract grows with the child

Children change; contracts must too. Individual contracts are versioned and re-agreed at defined points, with the child's own voice gaining weight as they mature. Old versions stay on record, so the terms a child was under at any time can always be answered for.

✦ Article 8Revocation is a right, exercised in one step

A family may end their contract at any time: the system stops, the child's data is returned or destroyed as the family directs, and there is no penalty and no obstacle course. Exit is a clause, not a customer-service ticket.

✦ Article 9This Charter cannot quietly change

The Charter is versioned and dated. A new version never silently rewrites an existing individual contract: families re-agree to it, or remain under the version they signed. And the contract outranks the model — nothing a MAVY system learns, and no personalization it develops, can amend its own contract.

✦ See the shapeThe individual contract, blank

What would a family actually sign? We publish the full structure with the blanks showing: the sample Individual Trust Contract. It is a template for negotiation — the real one is written with each family, and its requirements section is authored by the child's own clinicians and educators.

Comments on this draft are welcome from families, clinicians, educators, and researchers — write to research@mavericklabsresearch.org. The Charter will be better for the scrutiny, and scrutiny is the point of publishing it early.
