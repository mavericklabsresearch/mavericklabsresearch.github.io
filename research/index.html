✦ Our programmeThe MAVY research areas

MAVY's programme runs in five areas — environmental learning and hardware adaptation; persistent intelligence and experience retention; embodied robotics and sensorimotor learning; an independent safety architecture; and individualized, disability-adaptive assistance. A project pitch describing this programme was submitted to the NSF SBIR/STTR programme in November 2026; funding has not been established as awarded. Detail on each area: MAVY.

✦ Programme statusWhere the work stands

24 Nov 2026 · Trust

Draft Charter published for public comment

The MAVY Charter (Draft v0.1) and a blank sample Individual Trust Contract were published — the trust terms first, before capabilities, so families and reviewers can question them while they are still drafts.

24–25 Nov 2026 · Specifications

Trust and safety specifications drafted

Two internal working documents now stand behind the Charter: a Trust Contracts Specification (how contracts are structured, versioned, and checked) and a Safety Architecture Specification (how the deterministic safety layer keeps the contract's prohibitions). Both are v0.1 drafts in internal review; the Charter remains the public document of record until the review gate below is passed.

25 Nov 2026 · Mindset

The MAVY Mindset published

The lab published its founding position before its technology: MAVY is a mindset before it is a machine. The Mindset page (v1.0) sets out seven tenets — the person is the centre, trust before capability, the person keeps the decision — each tied to the document or mechanism that enforces it. A mindset with paperwork.

25 Nov 2026 · Requirements

Requirements format specified, tested against two simulated profiles

An internal Requirements Format Specification (v0.1) now defines how an individualized requirement is written so it can be scored met or missed, with satisfaction conditions fixed in advance — the section of each contract authored by a child's own clinicians and educators. It was worked end-to-end against two fully fictional simulated profiles. At this research stage the lab reports raw met/missed counts, not a percentage verdict: a compliance score measures whether the system kept its word, and says nothing about whether the intelligence itself is developing. Those are measured separately.

25 Nov 2026 · First machinery

Sentinel prototype built and run

The first working piece of the safety architecture — a deterministic policy checker compiled from a simulated profile's contract — was built and run against a test suite whose expectations were stated in advance: 13 of 13 scenario decisions as expected, 5 of 5 fault tests handled, including refusing an invented object location while permitting an honest "not found." Stated plainly: the checker and the expectations were written by the same hand, perception was simulated rather than sensed, and both halves ran in one process. This shows the design can be built as specified; it is not evidence the rules are the right ones. That judgement belongs to the review gate below.

26 Nov 2026 · The split made real

Two machines, one contract

The prototype was split across two real computers in the lab — the learning side on one machine, the safety checker on an NVIDIA AGX Orin — with every proposal judged over a cable between them. The same suites passed unchanged: the first profile's scenarios and faults, and a second simulated profile's full suite of 31 scenarios, including a street-crossing sequence in which the system may describe everything it senses but may never say "go" — the decision word stays the child's. A decision takes well under a millisecond; the checking will never be the slow part.

26 Nov 2026 · Senses, voice, and the diary

A first sense, a first word, a record that lasts

A camera on the safety checker's machine gave it a sense of its own: whether someone is actually present — presence only, never identity. In the first live test it refused a report of a person at a desk its own camera showed to be empty: it would not report what it could not see. Speech followed: the system now speaks only what the contract permits, aloud — its first words were a contracted phrase, spoken exactly as written, while a louder attempt and a friendlier rewording were both refused into silence. And the record of every decision now persists, survives restarts with its tamper-evident seal verified, and can be rendered in plain language a family can read. These are bench demonstrations in a laboratory, not a home, and not a product.

Next · In order

The review gate, and what remains

Before any Charter version becomes binding, it goes to attorney review and to reviewers from the disability community it governs. That gate is a commitment, not a formality: nothing is in force until it is passed. Alongside it: perception deeper than presence. Two items named open in the last update have since closed, and the closures are built, not just decided: the learning side may receive updates only as signed packages on physical media, applied by a guardian while the system is stopped for maintenance — a package with one altered byte was refused, and the refusal itself was recorded — and the decision record now controls who may read it by role, and keeps a record of every reading, granted or refused. The programme's working records are kept in the lab's private repository; what is public is what this page states.
