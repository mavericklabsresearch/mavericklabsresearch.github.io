✦ Our primary initiative

MAVY: an intelligence that develops around the person

MAVY is a personalized synthetic intelligence for individualized assistance to people with disabilities. The premise is simple to say and hard to build: the child is the developmental center. The intelligence persists and develops through experience, while the models, hardware, and robot bodies underneath it can be swapped as technology improves. Assistive intelligence should develop around the individual — not require the individual to adapt to it.

✦ The programmeFive research areas

Area A

Learning its environment

Environmental learning and hardware adaptation: the system learns the spaces and devices it actually runs on, including constrained hardware, instead of assuming a perfect data-center world.

Area B

Remembering what matters

Persistent intelligence and experience retention: verified knowledge and capabilities should survive shutdowns and model swaps, so a child's assistant does not start over every morning.

Area C

A body it can learn

Embodied robotics and sensorimotor learning: engineers set the physical configuration and limits; MAVY develops coordination within them.

Area D

Safety that stands apart

An independent safety architecture: the generative intelligence is separated from deterministic safety controls, so the creative part can never overrule the protective part. See Safety.

Area E

Fitted to one person

Individualized, disability-adaptive assistance: support shaped to a specific person's predefined requirements — tested first against simulated profiles, not on children.

The long view

The crossing

Our defining long-term demonstration, stated as an aspiration and nothing more: a blind autistic child safely navigating a downtown Kansas City pedestrian crossing with MAVY's assistance. We are not there. The road to it is the research.

✦ StatusWhat is true today

MAVY is an intended product and architecture at an early research/feasibility stage. The research programme behind it — its areas, stages, and open questions — is set out on our Research page. No complete autonomous synthetic-intelligence system has been demonstrated, and nothing on this site should be read as claiming one.

How to read this page. Everything here is educational information about a field, written in plain language. It is not medical advice, and it is not a claim about any product — including ours. MAVY is at an early research stage and its central capabilities have not yet been validated.
