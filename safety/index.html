✦ Our commitments

Safety is not a feature. It is a separate system.

A generative AI is, by construction, a system that produces plausible output — including plausible mistakes. Putting such a system near a vulnerable child and hoping it behaves is not a safety strategy. Ours is architectural: safety lives in a separate system the AI cannot talk its way around.

✦ Principle oneSeparation

In MAVY's design, the generative intelligence is separated from deterministic safety controls. Prohibited actions and predefined faults are handled by rules that do not learn, do not improvise, and do not take instructions from the generative side. The creative part of the system can propose; the safety part disposes.

✦ Principle twoHumans stay in charge

Caregivers and professionals define each child's requirements and limits. The system works inside them. Overrides, reviews, and shutdowns belong to people, and a system that cannot be switched off by the adults responsible for a child should never be switched on.

✦ Principle threePrivacy by design

A child's data is not a resource to be mined; it is a trust to be held. Our design commitments: collect the minimum, keep processing on the child's own hardware wherever possible, make records visible to the family, and make deletion real. These are commitments we state now so we can be held to them later.

✦ Principle fourSimulated first, always

New capabilities are tested against simulated profiles and controlled environments before any question of involving a real child arises — and any future work with children would require the full apparatus of independent ethical review and informed consent. MAVY's current research involves no children.

✦ Principle fivePublish the failures

Maverick Labs publishes its failed experiments at the same size as its passes, under criteria fixed in advance — and applies the same rule to safety-relevant results: a safety system whose failures are hidden is marketing, not engineering. Our working reference for child-centred AI is UNICEF's Guidance on AI and Children, whose requirements — safety, privacy, fairness, transparency, inclusion — we treat as a floor, not a ceiling.
